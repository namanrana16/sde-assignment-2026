# Monte Carlo of the experiment decision rule (C6) and decay response (C7)

**Provenance (read first).** Regenerated at the round-5 code (decay-epoch fix `23aa375`, CUSUM reference cap and `EXP_DECAY_ALPHA` = 0.05) with `python scripts/mc_null.py --runs 800 --power-runs 300 --decay-runs 250`. Earlier versions of this file were misleading in two ways, both found in review rounds 4 and 5:

1. The version committed until round 4 was generated at `b0c172f`, before later experiment changes, and was never re-run.
2. Until round 5 the Monte Carlo passed `exp_decay.decide` a holdout view without the recent holdout and winner connect counts, so `EXP_ROLLBACK_HOLDOUT_MIN` always put the CUSUM route on hold. The rows of the round-4 version therefore measured the sequential route only. The real controller supplies those counts, so its CUSUM route was live.

What each stage measured (decaying winner at approval 2 h; stable winners at approval 8 h; same seeds, 250 runs; rows marked "dev run" are scratch runs, not committed):

| code / Monte Carlo | decaying winner: rolled back of promoted, exposure-days | stable winner falsely rolled back, of promoted: +5% / +8% / +15% |
|---|---|---|
| before `23aa375`, CUSUM route masked (round-4 provenance row) | 30% (7.2 of 24.0), 5.72 | not measured |
| after `23aa375`, CUSUM route masked (round-4 report) | 97%, 2.73 | - / - / 3.8% |
| after `23aa375`, CUSUM route live (faithful; the controller's real behaviour then; dev run) | 98%, 1.20 | 70.0% / 70.7% / 40.9% |
| + CUSUM reference capped at +3% (dev run) | 98%, 1.73 | 20.0% / 12.2% / 3.8% |

Cause of the stable-winner false rollbacks: winner's curse. The CUSUM reference was the winner's own last 48 h before promotion, which overlaps the window that selected it, so a winner regressing to its true level read as decay. The reference is now capped at `1 + EXP_CUSUM_REF_CAP_LIFT` (3%, the minimum promotion lift). The remaining false rollbacks came from the sequential route at alpha 0.10 per epoch with restarting epochs; 0.05 halved them for 3-7% more decay exposure. The +5% and +8% rows rest on 20 and 41 promoted runs and are noisy. public_01 and public_02 grades are byte-identical before and after.

Generated by `python scripts/mc_null.py`; applies `controller.exp_rules`, `exp_alloc` and `exp_decay` unchanged at 4-hour tick resolution (looks at each day's end).

Setup: 3000 dials/day at pickup 0.3 (about 900 connects/day), engagement 0.5, 4500 leads with intra-lead correlation 0.1; one control and two (ramp mix: three) challengers entering at 7.5% (ramp to 20% after >= 1 day and >= 100 connects at point >= control; drop on the whole-experiment z <= -1.5 below 300 connects, <= -1 above); one workflow test (alpha 0.020000000000000004) and two half-traffic analysis groups (alpha 0.0025000000000000005 each, 0.025 per dim). Contrasts are Mantel-Haenszel pooled over strata of day x share regime. Winner: <= 4 Lan-DeMets OBF looks at information 0.25/0.5/0.75/1, Holm, point lift >= 3%, recent-24h one-sided 95% lower bound > 0, pickup non-inferiority. Promotion is two-stage: stage 1 winner 50% / old control 50%; stage 2 90/10 after >= 24 h at stage 1 with a positive recent lower bound and concurrent contrast. Every approved change waits an approval latency, then is re-checked (promotion stages: recent-window one-sided 80% lower bound > 0; ramp: z and recent z; drop: still at the drop bound) and applied or withheld. Decay: sequential winner-vs-control test (alpha 0.05 per epoch, Lan-DeMets OBF, epoch = information for a -15% effect at 80% power) plus CUSUM confirmed by the holdout direction. The harm-stop (removal only) is omitted, which is conservative.

False-winner rate under the null (any arm, any scope): **1.1%** (target <= 5%). A null scenario has 4 experiments (2 workflows x 2 dims), so the chance of at least one false promotion is about 4x this. The unstratified drift row is the comparison: at a +-5% drift the two false-winner rates are indistinguishable (the drift averages out over day-end looks and the shares move little before a winner is declared), so stratification costs no size or power here and only guards the contrast against confounding when shares and the base rate move together.
